The TIDE Glossary is a comprehensive list of acronyms and terms used at Tide, covering a wide range of topics including financial products, regulatory terms, marketing metrics, and operational processes.

It includes detailed definitions for key concepts such as KYB (Know Your Business), KYC (Know Your Customer), various member statuses like Active, Engaged, Dormant, and Churned, and product usage frameworks that track member engagement over different time periods.

The glossary also explains internal roles, marketing and acquisition metrics like CPKM (Cost Per KM), and operational tools and frameworks used within Tide.

Additionally, it outlines regulatory bodies and compliance terms such as FCA, AML, and MLRO, as well as technical terms related to payment systems and data governance.

The Platform and Product Usage Framework categorizes member and user engagement into stages such as Ready, Engaged, Active, Sticky, Inactive, Dormant, and Churned, providing a structured approach to understanding customer interaction with Tide's products and services.

Deflection Rate (Member Support)

Member Support Overall Deflection rate refers to the percentage of all support tickets that are self-served by members (via our BOTS) and not escalated to our human agents.

Formula: (All tickets where agents are not involved - Members come back in 24 hrs with same enquiry) / (Total Tickets Raised)

Example: if in a month we have 100 total tickets raised: 
A. 20 are resolved via the chatbots (and members answers: YES to :“Has this resolved your issue?”)
B. 10 are going through the chatbots, but the member does not answer the question (so abandons the ticket)
C. All tickets where agents are not involved is A+B which is 30 = 20+10
D. Out of the above, 3 members come back in 24 hours raising the same issue
E. 70 are escalated to our agents
This means our deflection rate is : (A+B-D)/ 100 = (20+10-3)/ 100= 27%

KYB




Know Your Business

Process of performing Customer Due Diligence measures on businesses entering into relationship with Tide. This includes obtaining identification data, performing data verification and conducting applicable screening activities (Sanctions, Blocklist) 

A process of Tide verifying the identity of its clients and assessing potential risks of illegal intentions for relationship. KYB relates to the verifications of Businesses; Tide has three levels of KYB for businesses:

Levels

No KYB (Non-member) "Not verified"
e.g. business recipient of an invoice written by a Tide member assigned a Tide BusinessID (assigned a Tide BusinessID in the process)

KYB0 - “Lobby state”

e.g. a business using non-regulated products or regulated products where regulated functionalities are restricted to draft mode only (including 'lobby state')

KYBa - "Light verified - for view usage" 
e.g. a business using products associated with auxiliary functionalities related to third-party bank accounts or non-account-based products, where Tide's relationship with a member is externally visible to third parties outside of member’s business and where a product could not be used to solicit a criminal activity, but could cause reputational risk to Tide (e.g, Funding Cloud product). linking a third party BCA into Tide for read-only; cashflow insights

KYBb - "Partially verified - for transactional usage" 
e.g. a business using products associated with auxiliary functionalities related to third-party bank accounts or non-account-based products, where Tide's relationship with a member is externally visible to third parties outside of member’s business and the product could potentially be used to facilitate criminal activity (e.g., Tide’s logo appearing on an invoice sent to a member’s underlying client). linking a third party BCA into the Tide to initiate payments or send an invoice

KYBc - "Fully verified - regulatory KYB"  
e.g. for Tide BCA (or any other product requiring full legal KYC)

KYBd - “Fully verified - regulatory KYB” (UK only)
e.g. for COSEC, COFO where Tide is ACSP

Country specificity

Provider specificity

Year-over-year (YOY) is a method of evaluating two values of the same metric, usually from a period of the current year versus a comparable period from the year before.

YOY comparison can be useful for mature business; but can be misleading if there were significant non-repeating differences in the two periods, e.g. holidays falling on different dates → in this case LFL (Like for Like) comparison has to be found.

Product
(Relevant for all members and users)

The member or user is now fully setup to start using the product because all the product onboarding steps have been fully completed.



Why important? Members and users cannot activate on a product if they do not have the right setup and access to the product to start using the product.

Note: The member or user has not yet interacted with the product but may have just clicked in to view the product. 




Active members or users have interacted with the product so have experienced the core value of the product. The specific interaction(s) that would count as showing "active" usage of the product will vary for each product. 

For Members:
The AM metric is expressed at the product level e.g. AM (Tide BCA) or AM (Tide Workplace Pension). 

For Users:
The AU metric is expressed at the product level e.g. AU (Tide Tide Card Reader) or AM (Tide Expense Card). 



Why important? We want to get members and users to this point as quickly as possible to increase retention.

Note: It's a specific set of actions that indicates the user has successfully grasped the product's core utility. Focuses on the meaningful interactions a member or user has with a product.

Sticky members or users regularly interact meaningfully with a product. These members or users have gone beyond initial activation, and demonstrate ongoing use and perceived value. Each product will have different definitions for product engagement that combine a list of expected interactions over a specific timeframe that demonstrates habit building. 



Why important? High product engagement generally correlates with higher member and user satisfaction, loyalty, and increased retention.

Note: This metric is all about member and user stickiness/habit. Focuses on the depth, quality, and meaningfulness of interactions. It's about whether members and users are deriving value and interacting with the core functionalities that deliver that value. Stickiness (versus active) is a stronger predictor of long-term success, retention, and revenue.

Member or user is Inactive on a specific product for 30/60/90/180 days.



Member or user has stopped using a specific product completely. Churn represents the loss of a member or user and can happen for various reasons, for example dissatisfaction with the product, found better alternatives from competitors, or simply a change in their needs. 



Why important? Tracking churn rate is crucial especially those products with subscription models, as it directly impacts revenue and indicates areas for improvement in product or customer experience.
